# Historical attribution and scope

The separated-translates mechanism used in the earlier large-gap argument is an instance of Jordan's lemma. A precise modern reference is Ignacio García-Marco, Pascal Koiran, and Timothée Pecatte, *On the linear independence of shifted powers*, Journal of Complexity 45 (2018), 67–82, Lemma 1.2, [arXiv:1705.03842](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03842), [DOI 10.1016/j.jco.2017.11.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jco.2017.11.002). The historical treatment is Grace and Young, *The algebra of invariants* (1903), Appendix III, pp. 375–377.

In the modern lemma, taking maximum degree `N=m+E` and lower degrees `l_i=m` gives the earlier threshold `m >= (n-1)(E+1)`. That mechanism must not be presented as historically new.

The current order-four proof uses a separate classification and symbolic branch argument. No claim of historical novelty for the general four-row theorem is made. A bounded literature search is not proof that an earlier theorem does not exist.

The result concerns distinct real offsets and distinct nonnegative integer exponents in an uncolored 4-by-4 masked determinant. It does not establish arbitrary matrix size, color-conditioned ASNC, or P versus NP. Audit means a separate AI-assisted mathematical review and independent implementation, not human peer review or Lean verification.
