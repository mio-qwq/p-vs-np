# Independent audit: order-four real consecutive-exponent theorem

Audit date: 18 October 2026 (UTC).

## Verdict and precise scope

PASS. I found no mathematical or computational gap in the frozen theorem:

For every binary 4 × 4 mask η with a perfect matching, every four distinct real offsets a₀,a₁,a₂,a₃, and every nonnegative integer d, the polynomial

    det[ηᵢⱼ (x+aᵢ)^(d+j)]  (j=0,1,2,3)

is not identically zero.

The audit independently reconstructs all 207 nontrivial symbolic cases and checks every one of the 65,536 labeled binary masks for coverage. It does not import the producer's checker, use the producer's exploratory classifier, use SymPy, use numerical samples, or rely on floating-point computation. The supplied order-at-most-three proof was checked and its finite support assertion was independently enumerated. Its proof works over the reals as needed here, although the supplied note initially states rational offsets.

The scope is strictly consecutive column exponents in order four, together with the stated support-component corollary. Gapped order-four exponents and larger matching-covered components are not established. The complex-offset extension is false. Nonvanishing is a statement about the polynomial, not a statement that it has no roots.

## Mathematical reductions checked

### Row permutations and affine normalization

Sorting row support codes only permutes rows and their associated offsets together. It changes the determinant by at most a sign. Equal support codes impose no equality of offsets. The symbolic normalized analysis quantifies over all allowed b,c, so no offset order or sign assumption has entered this reduction.

Let s=a₁−a₀ and β=(0,1,b,c), with b=(a₂−a₀)/s and c=(a₃−a₀)/s. Distinctness ensures s≠0. Substitution x=s y−a₀ gives the exact identity

    P_d(s y−a₀) = s^(4d+6) ∏ᵢ(y+βᵢ)^d · det[ηᵢⱼ(y+βᵢ)^j].

The scalar and row-factor product are nonzero polynomials, and the affine change of variable is invertible. Thus identically-zero status is preserved. The normalized distinctness condition is exactly

    D(b,c)=b c (b−1)(c−1)(c−b) ≠ 0.

No rationality restriction remains: b,c may be arbitrary real numbers satisfying this inequality. Factoring the common d first is valid because the polynomial ring over the reals is an integral domain.

### Top coefficient and allowed edges

For exponents 0,1,2,3, each supported permutation product is monic of total degree six. The degree-six coefficient is therefore exactly the binary mask determinant. The audit computes this scalar determinant independently by Laplace minors.

An edge is allowed precisely when deleting its row and column leaves a support with a perfect matching. The audit uses these smaller permanent computations, rather than the producer's union of supported permutations, to construct the allowed-edge graph. It verifies that this deletion leaves the matching count and scalar determinant unchanged.

At the polynomial level the justification is exact: any deleted edge occurs in no supported permutation term. Deleting edges cannot create a new supported permutation, so the entire polynomial determinant is unchanged. Every connected component of the remaining graph is balanced, because a perfect matching partitions into perfect matchings inside those components. Separate row and column permutations make the matrix block diagonal. If disconnected in order four, each block has at most three rows. Its exponents are an arbitrary subset of 0,1,2,3, which is why the arbitrary-exponent smaller-order lemma is needed.

### Order-at-most-three lemma

The supplied note's proof extends verbatim from rational offsets to all distinct offsets in a characteristic-zero field, in particular to real and complex offsets.

For order two, a unique matching gives one nonzero product. If there are two matchings, their monic products cannot be identical: distinct offsets give distinct linear roots, and distinct exponents give different root multiplicity profiles.

For order three, all 512 labeled binary masks were independently enumerated. Counts by number of perfect matchings are:

    r=0:265; r=1:150; r=2:69; r=3:18; r=4:9; r=6:1.

All nine r=4 masks have exactly eight entries present, hence are the full mask with one entry deleted. Odd r gives a nonzero top coefficient. For r=2, equal signs give a nonzero top coefficient, and opposite signs cannot cancel by the root-multiplicity argument.

For the full 3 × 3 determinant with distinct nonnegative exponents e₁,e₂,e₃ and S=Σeᵢ, expansion in powers of 1/x shows that degrees above S−3 vanish because repeated row powers have zero determinant. The coefficient at degree S−3 is the product of the offset and exponent Vandermonde determinants divided by 0!1!2!, and is nonzero. Thus its degree is S−3.

Deleting one entry subtracts that entry times its signed full 2 × 2 cofactor. The cofactor has degree equal to the sum of its two exponents minus one, with nonzero leading coefficient given by the corresponding offset difference times exponent difference. The subtracted product therefore has degree S−1. It cannot cancel the full determinant of degree S−3. This verifies the final r=4 case and justifies every small diagonal block.

## Polynomial certificate audit

The audit computes each normalized determinant by recursively expanding Laplace minors. Entries are constructed independently from their binomial coefficients as sparse polynomials in x,b,c. The resulting seven b,c coefficient polynomials are compared exactly, term for term, against every stored coefficient in the 207-case certificate.

The producer's rational polynomial arithmetic was reviewed:

- Sparse addition and multiplication use exact Fraction arithmetic.
- Its bivariate division chooses the lexicographically leading monomial. Lexicographic order on nonnegative exponent pairs is an admissible monomial order; the ordinary multivariate division algorithm terminates, and each quotient/remainder step preserves p=q·quotient+remainder.
- Its repeated stripping is only of the five factors known to be nonzero on D≠0. Whether factors are removed in a particular order is immaterial to the zero-set equivalence on that domain.
- Its dense univariate Euclidean algorithm returns a monic gcd using exact rational divisions. The zero-polynomial cases are handled, and the final substituted coefficient list is explicitly required not to be identically zero.

To avoid relying on the producer's division code, the audit strips the five factors by synthetic division, treating one variable as the main variable and the other as a polynomial coefficient. It checks every division reconstruction and each original coefficient's full factorization identity. In particular, division by c−b is performed as a polynomial in c, unlike the producer's lexicographic division, which leads in b. Exact divisibility still has a unique quotient, and the results agree.

For all 171 direct cases, a checked coefficient is a nonzero rational constant times a product of distinctness factors. Such a coefficient cannot vanish on D≠0.

In the other 36 cases the highest nonzero stripped coefficient is verified to be an affine linear polynomial L. Its stored parametrization is checked to describe the entire line L=0, not merely a subset: either b=t and c is the uniquely determined affine expression, or b is constant and c=t. Every stripped coefficient is independently substituted onto this line.

The audit computes the gcd with extended Euclid and stores explicit Bézout coefficients hⱼ satisfying

    Σⱼ hⱼ(t) pⱼ(t) = g(t).

It also checks that g divides every pⱼ. These identities certify that the common zeros of the substituted coefficients are exactly the zeros of g, over either the real or complex numbers. The audit verifies the certificate's exact g and the substituted D. Every removed gcd factor divides D, so it cannot vanish at an admissible parameter. It checks the cumulative factorization of g after removal.

For 35 cases, the remaining factor is a nonzero constant. For the one exceptional case it is exactly t²+t+1, and the completed-square identity is checked with rational arithmetic:

    t²+t+1 = (t+1/2)²+3/4 > 0 for real t.

## Exceptional case and negative controls

An independent four-variable symbolic Laplace expansion confirms the human-readable identity for row codes (6,15,15,15), with offsets 0,a,b,c:

    det = x(a−b)(a−c)(b−c)[ab+ac+bc+x(a+b+c)].

With distinct real offsets, a zero polynomial would force a+b+c=0 and ab+ac+bc=0. Hence a²+b²+c²=0, impossible for distinct real a,b,c. This agrees with the normalized quadratic obstruction and gives a direct proof of the only class requiring realness.

Eight corrupted-certificate controls are rejected:

1. Altered determinant coefficient
2. Missing class certificate
3. Duplicate class certificate
4. Falsified matching count
5. Falsified nonzero constant obstruction
6. Exceptional quadratic gcd replaced with a constant
7. Corrupted affine parametrization
8. Falsified exhaustive class count

Two false-claim controls are disproved exactly:

- Complex-offset extension: for offsets (0,1,t,−1−t) with t²+t+1=0, every determinant coefficient for (6,15,15,15) vanishes modulo t²+t+1. The distinctness polynomial reduces to −3−6t, whose gcd with t²+t+1 is 1. Thus the complex offsets are distinct and the determinant is zero.
- Repeated-offset extension: the full mask at offsets (0,1,0,2) has every coefficient zero, as expected from repeated rows.

## Gaps and trust boundaries

No gap was found within the stated real consecutive-exponent theorem or its balanced-support-component corollary. This is a checked finite symbolic proof with a human-readable explanation of the reductions, rather than a proof formalized in a proof assistant. Its execution trust base includes Python's standard-library exact integer/Fraction arithmetic and the short independent verifier; the algebraic identities and complete enumeration are available for further checking.

No public or third-party write, external download, or numerical parameter sampling was used. No claim about gapped exponents, arbitrary matching-covered order, or root-free determinants follows from this audit.
