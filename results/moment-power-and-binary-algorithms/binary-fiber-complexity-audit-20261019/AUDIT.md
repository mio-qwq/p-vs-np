# Complexity audit: complete binary matching fibers

19 October 2026. This is an independent audit of the exact affine criterion in `matching-fiber-geometry-20261019/PROOF.md`. It supplies representation-sensitive complexity statements, a rigorous succinct-input reduction, and bit bounds. It does not establish a complexity-class separation, a general deterministic synthesis algorithm, or a noncancellation theorem.

## 1. Results at a glance

Let G=(A,B,E) be a simple bipartite graph, |A|=|B|=n, m=|E|, X=PM(G), and let nonempty F be the desired complete fiber.

* If F is an explicit list, verifying a proposed binary coloring is in coNP and in coRP. Binary-fiber existence is consequently in MA, as well as the elementary upper bound Sigma_2^P. These are upper bounds, not completeness claims.
* If F is instead specified by a polynomial-size Boolean membership circuit, binary-fiber recognition is coNP-hard even on a disjoint union of K2,2 blocks. The same reduction obstructs arbitrary real-cost realizability. This lower bound does not apply to the explicit-list problem.
* The real/integer equivalence in the source proof can be strengthened to a deterministic polynomial-time construction of polynomial-bit weights whose fiber is exactly the affine closure of F among matchings. This avoids its original potentially exponential hyperplane-avoidance search, but does not decide whether that closure equals F and does not produce binary weights.
* Enumerating all matchings is inherently exponential on some tiny-width graph families. Neither polynomial-delay enumeration nor polynomially bounded coefficients means graph-polynomial total running time.
* A valid restricted algorithm can parameterize the affine dimension d of the matching polytope: at most 2^d matchings exist, and attainable binary cost signatures can be computed in time polynomial in the input for every fixed d. The dependence (2n+1)^d is XP, not an FPT bound.

## 2. Precisely different input models

**Explicit F.** The input contains G and f distinct perfect matchings, each as a row-to-column permutation or an m-bit incidence vector. Reading the input costs at least its actual length, which can be exponential in n. Duplicate and malformed list entries should be rejected or normalized. Bounds polynomial in n+m+fn are polynomial in the explicit input, not necessarily in G alone.

**Complete X supplied.** The input additionally lists every perfect matching. Completeness of this list is either a promise or must be verified; silently trusting a purported list of X is not valid. Under a completeness promise a fixed binary coloring can be checked in O((f+|X|)n) arithmetic operations. Finding a coloring still solves a Boolean feasibility problem; no polynomial synthesis bound follows just from writing down its constraints.

**Succinct F.** A circuit C decides membership of a matching, or of its block-choice bit string for disjoint cycles. This representation can encode exponentially many matchings with polynomially many gates. The hardness in Section 5 is for this model only. A black-box membership oracle, a Boolean circuit, and an explicit list must not be conflated.

**Family generated by a cost formula.** If F is already defined as the complete level set of a supplied binary coloring, existence is tautological; a separate intended family can still require equality checking. An arbitrary cancellation subset is not such a cost formula.

## 3. Elementary exhaustive bounds and certificate costs

There are at most n! perfect matchings and 2^m binary colorings. Enumerating all permutations and all colorings therefore gives a deterministic exact algorithm, but not an efficient one. A more sensible matching enumeration uses a prefix tree on the ordered left vertices: extend a prefix only when the residual graph has a perfect matching, tested by an ordinary bipartite matching algorithm. There are at most 1+n|X| viable prefixes and at most n candidate children per prefix. This yields polynomial delay and total time (|X|+1)poly(n,m). It also permits checking a promise |X|<=Q by stopping on the (Q+1)-st output in (Q+1)poly(n,m) time.

The disjoint union of r copies of K2,2 has 4r edges and 2^r perfect matchings. Thus writing every matching already needs exponential output length. Bounded treewidth or cycle components of constant size do not eliminate this issue.

A binary witness w has m bits; t has O(log(n+1)) bits. Checking that every listed member of F has cost t is polynomial. Checking that no unlisted matching has cost t is a separate universal obligation. Failure has a polynomial certificate: an outside matching N with w.p_N=t, verified against the explicit list. Consequently fixed-color completeness is in coNP. This alone does not put synthesis in NP: the witness's full verification has not become deterministic polynomial time.

For a fixed coloring there is also a deterministic polynomial-time Turing reduction to Exact Matching. Construct the prefix trie of F. Each matching outside F has a first prefix edge that leaves this trie; there are at most O(fn^2) such prefixes. For each, remove the vertices fixed by the prefix and ask whether the residual graph has a perfect matching with the remaining red count. Reject completeness if any answer is yes. This is an upper reduction, not a hardness proof for the nonempty-explicit-F problem. With F empty, completeness is exactly the complement of Exact Matching, but the source theorem assumes nonempty F.

Minimum and maximum red counts are insufficient substitutes for the exact-count queries: a K2,2 with both diagonal edges red and both off-diagonal edges blue has achievable costs {0,2}, with a gap at 1.

## 4. Explicit-list verification in coRP; synthesis in MA

This result is self-contained except for the elementary polynomial zero-probability bound.

First reject a proposed witness if any listed matching fails validity or has a different red count. Set t to the common count. Introduce an independent variable x_e for each edge and z for red count. Define the n by n matrix

    A_ab(x,z) = x_(a,b) z^(w_(a,b)) for edges, and 0 otherwise.

Let

    Q(x) = [z^t] det A(x,z)
           - sum_(M in F) sgn(M) product_(e in M) x_e.

Distinct perfect matchings have distinct edge monomials. Hence every listed matching cancels exactly, and every outside target-count matching contributes its own nonzero coefficient, +1 or -1. Therefore Q is identically zero if and only if F is the complete target fiber. This assertion remains true over every prime field, including characteristic two; no two distinct edge monomials coalesce merely by reducing coefficients.

Q has total degree n. Choose a prime 6n<p<12n (handle n=0 directly). Let b=floor(log_2 p), and independently sample each x_e uniformly from S={0,...,2^b-1} inside F_p, using exactly b random bits per edge. Since |S|>p/2>3n, the Schwartz-Zippel bound gives Pr[Q(x)=0] <= n/|S| < 1/3 for a nonzero Q. A zero Q always evaluates to zero. Using this power-of-two sample set avoids any unbounded rejection-sampling loop in the worst-case polynomial-time verifier.

The evaluation is polynomial-time: det A(x,z) has z-degree at most n, so evaluate n+1 numerical determinants at distinct field elements z, interpolate, and extract its t-th coefficient. This takes O(n^4+fn+n^2) field operations using ordinary cubic determinant elimination. A prime between 6n and 12n exists for n>=1 by Bertrand's theorem and can be located deterministically in polynomial time even by trial division. All field elements have O(log(n+1)) bits.

The algorithm accepts completeness when Q(x)=0. True completeness is always accepted; false completeness is accepted with probability below 1/3. Thus fixed-color completeness is in coRP, and its complement is in RP. This is randomized verification, not a deterministic certificate or a substitute for exact verification of a reported result.

For synthesis, Merlin supplies w, then Arthur independently chooses the random field values and runs this verifier. A realizable F has a witness accepted with probability one. If no witness is valid, every proposed w is accepted with probability below 1/3. Therefore explicit-list binary-fiber synthesis is in MA, with perfect completeness for this protocol. Independently, the formula exists w for all matching encodings N, requiring cost equality exactly for F, gives the elementary Sigma_2^P upper bound.

This randomized procedure relies on subtracting an explicit sum of f monomials. A membership circuit for F does not supply that sum efficiently. The coRP/MA conclusions here cannot be transferred automatically to circuit-described F.

For background on the polynomial identity bound, see J. T. Schwartz, *Fast Probabilistic Algorithms for Verification of Polynomial Identities*, JACM 27(4), 1980, [DOI 10.1145/322217.322225](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/322217.322225), [author paper copy](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~cs787-1/Schwartz1980.pdf). Our algebraic argument does not depend on any claim about the current best deterministic Exact Matching algorithm.

## 5. Rigorous hardness for succinct membership circuits

**Theorem.** When a nonempty F is specified by a Boolean membership circuit, recognizing a complete binary fiber is coNP-hard, even when G is the union graph of F and is a disjoint union of K2,2 blocks. The same holds for complete real-cost fibers. Fixed-color completeness for circuit F is coNP-complete on this class, even for the all-zero coloring.

**Reduction.** Given a Boolean circuit C on r inputs x, construct r+2 disjoint K2,2 blocks. Encode each perfect matching by its choice vector (x,y_1,y_2) in {0,1}^(r+2). Define

    F_C = {(x,y_1,y_2): (y_1,y_2)!=(1,1) or C(x)=0}.

This membership circuit has size O(size(C)+r). The all-zero vector and every standard unit vector lie in F_C, regardless of C. Thus F_C is nonempty; its affine span is the entire block-choice space. Moreover these vectors use both local matching choices in every block, so the union of F_C is exactly G.

Every additive edge cost, real or binary, has the form

    c(x,y) = a + sum_j delta_j (x,y)_j.

Constancy on zero and every standard unit vector forces delta_j=0 for every j. Therefore any cost constant on F_C is constant on all perfect matchings. If C is unsatisfiable, F_C is all matchings and the zero coloring realizes it. If C is satisfiable, at least one matching (x,1,1) is excluded, so no additive cost can realize exactly F_C. Hence C is unsatisfiable if and only if F_C is realizable. This is a polynomial many-one reduction from Circuit-UNSAT, establishing coNP-hardness.

For the all-zero coloring and t=0, completeness is exactly whether F_C includes all matching choices, again Circuit-UNSAT. Fixed-color failure for arbitrary circuit F has a polynomial certificate consisting of a mismatching matching and a circuit evaluation, so the verification problem is also in coNP.

The reduction is deliberately representation-sensitive: F_C contains at least three quarters of all 2^(r+2) matchings. Writing F_C explicitly would take exponential space. It proves no explicit-F hardness result, no NP-hardness result for ordinary Exact Matching, and no P-versus-NP separation. A polynomial circuit-input algorithm would imply P=NP through this reduction; we do not assert the antecedent or its negation.

## 6. Polynomial-bit witnesses in the real-cost theorem

Assume the affine criterion holds and F is explicitly listed. Form the difference matrix D with rows p_M-p_M0, and let rho=rank(D), d=m-rho. Entries belong to {-1,0,1}. Choose rho independent rows and rho pivot columns, forming an invertible square matrix B. For each free column c, make a nullspace vector whose free coordinate is det(B), whose other free coordinates vanish, and whose pivot coordinates solve Bv=-det(B)c.

Cramer's rule shows every nonzero coordinate is a determinant of a rho by rho matrix with entries in {-1,0,1}. Hadamard's bound gives absolute value at most H=max(1,rho^(rho/2)). The d resulting integer vectors form a real basis of ker D. When rho=0 use the standard coordinate basis and H=1.

Let q=|X\F|. If q=0 use w=0. Otherwise the source proof finds

    w(s) = b_1+s b_2+...+s^(d-1)b_d,
    0<=s<=q(d-1).

Because q<=n!, log_2(max(1,s)) is O(n log(n+1)+log(m+1)). Each coordinate of w has absolute value at most d H max(1,s)^(d-1), so its bit length is

    O(log(d+1) + rho log(rho+1)
      + (d-1)[n log(n+1)+log(m+1)]).

This is polynomial in the graph size, despite potentially exponential numeric magnitude. Shifting all coordinates by the negative of the minimum, when needed, gives nonnegative integer weights with the same polynomial bit bound; the target needs only an additional O(log(n+1)) bits.

It does NOT follow that this original search over s runs in polynomial time. Searching all candidate s can take q(d-1)+1 trials, and testing each s for complete fiber equality is another nontrivial task. However the following large-radix construction eliminates the search entirely.

### 6.1 Deterministic large-radix construction of the affine closure fiber

**Theorem.** From explicitly listed nonempty F, one can construct in deterministic polynomial time nonnegative integer edge costs of polynomial bit length such that their target fiber in PM(G) is exactly PM(G) intersect aff(F). No enumeration of PM(G) is needed to construct these weights.

Use the integer nullspace basis b_1,...,b_d above and any explicit common coordinate bound H>=1, for example the maximum absolute coordinate of that computed basis, enlarged to at least one. Set

    C = 2nH,    s = C+2,    w = sum_(i=1)^d s^(i-1) b_i.

For every matching N, v=p_N-p_M0 has at most 2n nonzero entries, each +1 or -1. Hence a_i=b_i.v is an integer satisfying |a_i|<=C. If some a_i is nonzero, choose the largest nonzero index k. When k=1 the sum is immediately nonzero. Otherwise its leading term has magnitude at least s^(k-1), while all smaller terms together have magnitude at most

    C(1+s+...+s^(k-2)) = C(s^(k-1)-1)/(s-1) < s^(k-1).

Therefore w.v cannot vanish. Conversely all a_i=0 clearly implies w.v=0. Thus

    w.(p_N-p_M0)=0 iff p_N-p_M0 lies in (ker D)-perp
                          iff p_N lies in aff(F).

The basis is computable by exact rational elimination and the determinant scaling already described. The integer C and s have O(log(n+1)+rho log(rho+1)) bits, and every coordinate of w has

    O(log(d+1)+d[log(n+1)+rho log(rho+1)])

bits. Exponentiation, summation, and a uniform shift to nonnegative weights are all polynomial-time bit operations. The empty-basis case uses w=0 and the same orthogonality characterization; n=0 is handled directly.

If the affine closure criterion holds, this is a constructive polynomial-time witness algorithm for the real/integer theorem, even though recognition of that criterion has not been settled here. If it fails, the constructed weights expose precisely the extra affine-forced matchings. Detecting or listing those matchings remains separate. The exponentially large numerical value is harmless for binary encoding, but these weights are not binary edge colors. Expanding them into unary-sized gadgets would lose the polynomial bound.

Similarly, an affine obstruction N in aff(F) has a polynomial-size rational certificate. Choose at most m+1 affinely independent members of F whose augmented vectors (p_M,1) span (p_N,1). Cramer's rule on a full-rank square minor bounds numerator and denominator bit length by O(m log(m+1)). Verify the exact rational equality, the coefficient sum one, and N outside F. Thus failure of the real-cost affine criterion has a polynomial certificate in the explicit-list model. Negative coefficients are allowed and essential.

## 7. A valid restricted tractability route

Remove edges belonging to no perfect matching; these can be identified by forcing each edge and running ordinary bipartite matching. Suppose X is nonempty. Let E* be the remaining edges and c the number of connected components of (A,B,E*). The affine dimension of its perfect matching polytope is

    d = |E*| - 2n + c.

Indeed the bipartite degree-equation matrix has rank 2n-c. The polytope is exactly the nonnegative solutions of those equations, by bipartite matching integrality. The average of all perfect matchings is strictly positive on E*, so no extra affine equality is introduced by nonnegativity.

Every d-dimensional affine subspace of R^m has a coordinate projection onto d coordinates that is injective on it: select d independent rows of a direction-space basis matrix. Thus such a subspace contains at most 2^d Boolean vectors, and |X|<=2^d. In particular bounded d implies bounded total matching count, although the graph may have arbitrarily many forced/path edges.

Enumerate X in output-sensitive time and choose d independent difference rows p_M-p_M0. Let B be the resulting d by m matrix. For a binary coloring w define z=Bw. Each coordinate of z is in [-n,n]. Process the columns of B by a subset-sum dynamic program, choosing whether to include each column. At every prefix each coordinate remains in [-n,n], since each row has at most n entries +1 and at most n entries -1. There are at most (2n+1)^d states; store predecessor pointers to reconstruct one coloring per reachable state.

All differences p_N-p_M0 are rational linear combinations of the rows of B. Compute the combinations exactly. For each reachable z, test whether those linear combinations vanish exactly on F. A valid state gives a binary witness by backtracking; if none works, no binary witness exists. The state space, matching enumeration, exact linear algebra, and constraint testing all have cost

    (2^d + (2n+1)^d) poly(n,m,f,2^d),

with polynomial-bit arithmetic. For every fixed d this is a deterministic polynomial-time recognition-and-construction algorithm. It is an XP parameter bound; describing n^d as FPT would be incorrect. The case d=0 has a unique perfect matching and is handled directly.

This route extends beyond disjoint K2,2, but does not cover arbitrary bounded-treewidth graphs: r independent K2,2 blocks already have d=r. Any stronger claimed restriction should be checked against this example.

### 7.1 An additional class with unbounded affine dimension

The parallel constructive derivation supplied the following distinct sufficient condition, independently checked here.

**Selector-vertex criterion.** Suppose there is a left vertex a such that each incident edge belongs to at most one perfect matching. Then every nonempty F subset of X is a complete binary fiber. Color red exactly the edges at a that occur in F; color every other edge blue; use target t=1. Every matching has exactly one edge at a, and the hypothesis makes the map from a matching to this edge injective. Therefore it has cost one exactly when it is in F.

The graph property is recognizable in polynomial time without knowing X in advance. For every edge (a,b), delete its endpoints and find a residual perfect matching if one exists. Zero residual matchings means the edge is unused. If a residual matching exists, it is unique exactly when there is no alternating cycle: orient its matching edges from right to left and its other edges from left to right, then test for a directed cycle. This decides whether the original edge occurs in zero, one, or at least two perfect matchings. Test whether some left vertex has no incident edge in the last category. With an elementary O(nm) augmenting-path matching procedure, the total over all m edges is O(nm^2), apart from harmless lower-order graph scanning. For tiny empty residual graphs, the empty matching is unique.

This condition implies |X|<=deg(a)<=n, but allows unbounded affine dimension. For example take b>=2 internally vertex-disjoint odd-length paths between two opposite-partition terminals, with at most one length-one path to preserve graph simplicity. On each branch a perfect matching either covers both terminals or neither; exactly one branch covers both. There are exactly b perfect matchings, distinguished by the edge at one terminal. Every edge is allowed and the connected graph's affine dimension is b-1. Thus the criterion handles a connected family with arbitrarily large dimension, unlike the fixed-d algorithm.

## 8. Audit conclusions and boundaries

1. The source theorem correctly distinguishes equal cost on F from equality with the complete fiber.
2. The binary constraint cannot be recovered by scaling the real/integer witness; the source four-block obstruction remains decisive.
3. An algorithm must name its input representation, structural parameter, total matching-generation cost, integer/rational bit bounds, and deterministic or randomized verification guarantee.
4. Small exhaustive checks establish only those finite instances. The supplied reductions and algebraic arguments establish the universal claims here.
5. None of this proves a universal ASNC statement, cancellation-free determinant specialization, P!=NP, or a general binary-fiber synthesis lower bound for explicit F.

`check.py` independently checks the succinct reduction over all truth tables on at most two variables, the determinant coefficient identity on all K3,3 binary colorings and all their actual target fibers, the missing-target gap example, and the affine-dimension cardinality bound on all 3 by 3 bipartite supports. It also checks every nonempty F on every selector-vertex instance among those supports, and the large-radix affine-closure construction for every nonempty F on K3,3.
